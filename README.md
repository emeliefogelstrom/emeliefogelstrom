## 🧬 Origin Story

I've been curious about how things work since before the internet was something people had at home. When my dad brought home a Silicon Graphics workstation in the mid-90s, my siblings played games. I found the browser.

My first websites were about horses and dogs — because those were my worlds, and building something about what you love is the fastest way to learn. The subjects changed. The curiosity didn't.

Today I live as close to self-sufficient as modern life allows — growing food, raising animals, fixing things rather than replacing them. Most of my projects aren't abstract portfolio pieces. They solve problems I actually have:

A dog that shouldn't be left alone in a hot car. A livestock area that needs monitoring at night. Rats in the barn that got too comfortable. Plants that need water when I'm not watching.

I'm drawn to the detective work in development — the moment when you understand why something isn't working, and the quiet satisfaction when it does. That part never gets old.
